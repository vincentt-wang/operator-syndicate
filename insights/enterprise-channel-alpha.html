Why Enterprise Channel Access Is the Real Alpha in B2B

By EvoScale Capital · 6 min read · June 2026
The argument

In B2B, capital is abundant and access is scarce. The startups that win enterprise revenue do not out-raise their competitors — they out-access them. Operator-led investing is how that access becomes systematic.

Talk to any B2B founder selling into the enterprise and you hear the same story. The product works, early users are happy, the metrics look good. Then the enterprise deal arrives and everything slows to a crawl: procurement, security review, legal, a buying committee the founder has never met. By most estimates, around four out of five B2B startups stall at exactly this point, and a typical enterprise sales cycle runs twelve to eighteen months. The product was never the problem. The closed door was.

Why the usual playbook fails

The standard response is to throw resources at the wall: raise a bigger round, hire more sales reps, buy more ads. But none of that opens a door that runs on trust and relationships. More runway just lets you wait longer outside it. As Andreessen Horowitz has argued for years, durable advantage lives in distribution, not the product alone — and Y Combinator drills the same point into every batch: you have to earn your go-to-market, you cannot buy it.

The answer: access is the alpha

Here is the asymmetry. Capital is abundant — there is far more money chasing B2B than there are deals worth backing. What is scarce is a warm path to the VP who owns the budget and the director who runs the evaluation. The product gets you considered; access gets you closed. That gap between abundant capital and scarce access is precisely where the alpha sits.

How operator-led investing delivers it

This is the gap EvoScale Capital is built to close. Our operators are senior executives — VP, Director, and GM level — at the exact companies a startup is trying to sell into. They do not just write checks; they know the decision-makers and open the door on day one, turning a cold cycle into a warm introduction. We call the fit between a deal and the operators who can move it Channel Fit, and it is screened on every deal.

Access alone is not a strategy without discipline. Every qualified deal first runs through a structured three-lens screen — desirability, feasibility, and viability — so operator LPs can weigh in within minutes. Once they reach consensus, the deal advances to the Investment Committee for formal due diligence. The early read stays fast and operator-led; the diligence stays rigorous. The point, echoing IDEO's human-centered lens, is to read each deal from the buyer's world, not the founder's pitch.

The takeaway

If you are an investor, the lesson is that B2B alpha increasingly comes from access you can supply, not just capital you can deploy. If you are a founder, the lesson is sharper still: choose the backer who shortens your path to the enterprise, not just the one who writes the largest check. In a market drowning in capital, the scarce resource — access — is the one worth optimizing for.
