Why Enterprise Channel Access Is the Real Alpha in B2B

By EvoScale Capital · June 2026

For most B2B startups, the hardest part is not building the product, and it is not even raising capital. It is getting into the room where enterprise buying decisions are actually made.

Founders feel this every day. The product works, early users are happy, the deck is sharp. Then comes the enterprise sale, and momentum stalls for twelve to eighteen months in procurement, security reviews, and committees the founder has never met. Capital does not fix this. More runway just lets you wait longer outside a door that will not open.

The bottleneck was never capital

The market is flush with money chasing B2B. What is scarce is access: a warm path to the VP who owns the budget, the director who runs the evaluation, the operator who can say ‘take this meeting.’ Roughly four out of five B2B startups stall at exactly this point, not because the product is weak, but because the channel is closed.

What the best investors have long understood

This is not new. Andreessen Horowitz has argued for years that distribution, not the product alone, is where durable advantage lives. Y Combinator drills the same lesson into every batch: talk to customers and earn your go-to-market the hard way. And IDEO’s human-centered design philosophy applies directly to enterprise selling: you win by understanding the buyer’s world, not by pushing harder.

Operator-led investing changes the odds

This is the gap EvoScale Capital is built to close. Our operators are senior executives, VP, Director, and GM level, at the exact companies a startup is trying to sell into. They do not just write checks. They know the decision-makers and open the door on day one, turning a cold twelve-to-eighteen-month cycle into a warm introduction. Every qualified deal first runs through a structured three-lens screen so operator LPs can weigh in within minutes; once they reach consensus, it advances to the Investment Committee for formal due diligence.

This could be your shortest path to several-million-dollar business.
